HPC and AI

Systems shaped around the research.

A useful system starts with understanding the work people need to do. I bring compute, storage, networks and scientific software together, and stay involved through the practical work of making them run well.

01

Cluster architecture and build-out

I help turn research needs into a working cluster: choosing hardware, planning capacity, installing systems and setting up the tools your team will use to manage them. My work includes Bright Cluster Manager, OpenHPC, HPCM and xCAT, across individual sites and larger environments.

02

InfiniBand and low latency fabric

When an analysis spans many machines, the connections between them matter. I design and troubleshoot InfiniBand and Ethernet networks, including OpenSM, NVIDIA UFM, RoCE and RDMA, and trace performance problems through the switches, adapters and applications.

03

Parallel and scale-out storage

Research data needs room to grow and a dependable path to the people and programs using it. I build and tune parallel storage, plan data movement and recovery, and test it with the actual workload. Systems I work with include IBM Storage Scale / GPFS, Lustre, WEKA, VAST, PowerScale / Isilon and DDN.

04

GPU, CUDA and AI stacks

I build GPU environments for model training, inference and scientific computing, with particular experience in NVIDIA systems: A100, H100, DGX, CUDA, NVLink and NCCL. The work includes matching memory, storage and networking to the job, then making PyTorch, TensorFlow and container environments usable for the team.

05

Schedulers and workload management

A shared cluster needs priorities that make sense to the people using it. I configure Slurm, PBS Pro and LSF, including queues, GPU allocations, fair share and usage accounting. I also help teams move to a new scheduler while keeping their research workflows in view.

06

Cloud and hybrid HPC

I help you work out where cloud computing fits your research and budget, then build the environment and move the work. That includes AWS ParallelCluster, FSx for Lustre, EFA and Azure HPC, as well as systems that connect cloud resources with the equipment you already own.

Regulated environments

Care with sensitive research data.

When research comes with additional security or data-handling requirements, I help your team build them into the system and its daily operation. The examples below describe the environments and frameworks I have worked with.

CUI

Federal and controlled unclassified information

NIST SP 800-171 across storage, scheduler and identity, with the system security plan and POA&M that go with it. NIST SP 800-53 on federal systems, FISMA responsibility as a named security officer, and CMMC readiness for defense subcontracts. DISA STIG and CIS Benchmark hardening on compute at scale. NIST SP 800-88 media sanitization on decommission. FIPS 140 validated cryptography where it is required, and the exemption argument where a validated module does not exist for the hardware.

NIH

Controlled-access research data

The NIH Genomic Data Sharing Policy and the dbGaP security best practices as they actually apply to a shared cluster: where controlled-access data may live, who may reach it, how access is logged, and how it is destroyed at the end of the approved period. NIH Data Management and Sharing plans, data use agreements and honest broker arrangements, IRB-driven access boundaries, and the awkward part — enforcing per-study separation on a filesystem several hundred other users also mount.

Export

Import, export and sanctions

EAR and ECCN classification of HPC hardware, GPUs and the software stack, and ITAR where it applies. Deemed export is the one that catches universities: giving a foreign national an account on a machine holding controlled technology is an export, and shared clusters are built to do exactly that. Technology control plans, restricted party and OFAC screening, Entity List constraints on accelerator procurement, and where the fundamental research exclusion genuinely applies rather than where people wish it did.

Health

Clinical and pharmaceutical

HIPAA Security Rule on estates that are simultaneously research and clinical, GxP and 21 CFR Part 11 for electronic records and signatures, PCI DSS, and GDPR where a collaboration crosses into the EU. Audit evidence that can be produced on demand rather than reconstructed the week before an assessment.

ISO

Standards and control frameworks

Systems designed and operated to ISO/IEC 27001 information security controls, ISO/IEC 27017 and 27018 for cloud and personal data in cloud, ISO/IEC 20000 service management practice, and SOC 2 control objectives. Where an organization is pursuing certification, the work is the control implementation and the evidence that survives the audit.

In practice

What this looks like on a cluster

Compliance fails on shared HPC in predictable places: a scratch filesystem nobody scoped, an epilog that never deletes, a scheduler prolog that leaks a path, home directories mounted everywhere by default, node images drifting apart, and audit logging that stops at the login node. The useful work is closing those, then proving they are closed — not producing a policy document that describes a system nobody has actually configured.
