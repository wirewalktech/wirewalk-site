    What we find

    The same eight findings, in organization after organization.

    None are exotic. They are what accumulates when a business grows faster than the controls around it, and none of them show up in a vulnerability scan. Each is detectable within a defined number of days, and each one has been the root cause of a public breach somewhere.

    Audits

    Nine scopes. Run one, or run them as a program.

    Each is scoped independently, with its own testing plan, evidence requirements and findings register. Most clients begin with identity and cloud, then extend. Every finding carries the control reference for whichever frameworks apply to you, so the register drops into work you already report on.

    How it works

    Five stages. Nothing in the report surprises you.

    Findings are walked through with the engineer or administrator who owns the system while testing is still open, so what reaches your desk has already survived contact with the people who run it. Anything that does not survive that conversation is withdrawn, and the report says so.

    Where the six weeks go

    Typical single-scope engagement. Testing varies 2–4 weeks with the size of the estate; shown at three.
    Day 90 — we return and re-test what was supposed to close.
    Stage 1

    Scope

    Half a day with the person accountable for security, the engineering lead and whoever owns the budget, to agree what is in scope, what is out, and what testing is authorized in writing.

    Half a day
    Stage 2

    Testing

    Configuration review, access and log analysis, and hands-on testing against the agreed scope, run from read-only accounts wherever a read-only account can answer the question.

    2–4 weeks
    Stage 3

    Validation

    Every finding is reproduced and walked through with the system owner, so the register reflects the environment rather than our first read of it.

    1 week
    Stage 4

    Report

    A findings register ranked by severity, each with reproduction steps, evidence, an owner, a remediation step and a date, plus a two-page summary.

    1 week
    Stage 5

    Re-test

    We return at ninety days and test what was supposed to close. An audit nobody re-tests is a document rather than a control.

    Day 90
    Severity

    Every finding arrives with a clock attached.

    Severity reflects exposure and exploitability, not how hard something is to fix. The window below is what we agree with you at scoping, and it is what the day-90 re-test measures against. A finding with no date is a finding nobody owns.
