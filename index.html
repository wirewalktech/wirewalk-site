HPC and AI

The part of the stack most people cannot hire for.

Compute, fabric and storage are one system, and the failures that matter happen between them rather than inside any one of them. I work across all three, from architecture and procurement through build-out, benchmarking and day-to-day operation.

01

Cluster architecture and build-out

Full stack design and delivery: hardware selection, provisioning, operating system and image management, node classes, and the capacity planning behind them. Bright Cluster Manager, OpenHPC, HPCM, xCAT. Multi-site and multi-region estates.

02

InfiniBand and low latency fabric

Fabric design and management from EDR through HDR and NDR, fat tree and dragonfly topologies, OpenSM routing engines, NVIDIA UFM, RoCE and RDMA. Switch and adapter firmware, port configuration, and the diagnostics that separate a fabric problem from an application problem.

03

Parallel and scale-out storage

GPFS and IBM Storage Scale, Lustre, WekaFS, VAST, Pure, Isilon, DDN, BeeGFS and CEPH. Tiering and policy, erasure coding, data movement, and benchmarking that measures the workload rather than the page cache.

04

GPU, CUDA and AI stacks

A100, H100, DGX systems, NVLink, DCGM, Intel Max and AMD Instinct. CUDA and ROCm, NCCL collective tuning, PyTorch and TensorFlow environments, containerized delivery through Apptainer and Kubernetes.

05

Schedulers and workload management

Slurm, PBS Pro, LSF, Grid Engine and Univa. Partition and QoS design, GPU and licence-aware scheduling, fair share, accounting, and the migrations between schedulers that organizations put off for years.

06

Cloud and hybrid HPC

AWS ParallelCluster, SOCA and IDEA, FSx for Lustre, EFA and the surrounding services. Azure HPC across regions. Lift and shift from on-premises, and the more common case where the honest answer is that only part of the workload should move.

Other areas

The infrastructure around the cluster.

HPC does not sit on its own. It depends on identity, network, storage, monitoring and compliance that have to work, and on people who can run them. AI is changing how each of these is done, and the shift is mostly practical rather than dramatic.

Enterprise IT and infrastructure

Linux and UNIX estates at scale, identity and directory services, virtualization, configuration management with Ansible and Puppet, monitoring through Prometheus, Grafana, Nagios and Ganglia. Migrations off legacy platforms, including Solaris and HP-UX, where the hard part is the application stack rather than the operating system.

Where AI changes it: telemetry has always been collected faster than anyone could read it. Anomaly detection across metrics and logs turns that backlog into something that surfaces problems before a user reports them, and capacity forecasting stops being a spreadsheet exercise.

Infrastructure

Low latency networking

Spine and leaf design, 10, 25 and 100 gigabit platforms, Juniper, Arista, Cisco and Brocade, OSPF and BGP, VXLAN and MLAG. Campus and transcontinental network optimization for research and engineering traffic that behaves nothing like office traffic.

Where AI changes it: traffic classification and fault correlation across a fabric this size is pattern work, and pattern work is where machine learning earns its place. It shortens the gap between a link degrading and someone knowing which link.

Network

Security, compliance and audit

HIPAA, GxP and PCI compliance in regulated research and clinical environments. SELinux and RBAC, vulnerability management, access and filesystem auditing, threat management across cloud and on-premises. NACI Public Trust clearance. Security officer responsibility on a federal modelling platform.

Where AI changes it: evidence gathering. Most audit effort goes into collecting and correlating what a control actually did, not into judgement. Automating that collection leaves more room for the part that needs a person.

Compliance

Data management and movement

Petascale storage planning, purchase and migration. Backup and archive across enterprise and cluster filesystems, tape libraries, snapshot and replication frameworks. Transfer at distance with Aspera and GridFTP. Retention and access control in environments where the data has legal weight.

Where AI changes it: placement. Deciding what belongs on flash, what belongs on capacity tiers and what should have been deleted is a prediction problem, and policy engines have historically guessed with very blunt rules.

Storage

Teams, vendors and budgets

Group lead and IT director roles: hiring and technical screening, mentorship, team structure, vendor and contract management, budgeting and expansion planning. Running engineering, development and support functions together rather than as separate silos.

Where AI changes it: mostly in what a small team can now cover. The practical question is which work it removes and which work it quietly creates, and that is worth answering before rather than after a reorganization.

Audits
